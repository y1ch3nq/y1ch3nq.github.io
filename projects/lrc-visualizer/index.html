01
Context

Built from inside the workflow.

The project grew from hands-on lyric-video production at AllSaints Music Group. Repeating the same timing, formatting, preview, and export steps made the real opportunity clear: the workflow needed a purpose-built product, not another editing workaround.

02
Problem

A creative task buried in repetitive production.

01

Manual timing

Lyric timing and line changes required repetitive, frame-by-frame attention.

02

Fragmented controls

Audio, lyrics, typography, color, layout, and format decisions lived across separate steps.

03

Slow revisions

Small visual changes could trigger another long editing and export cycle.

04

Inconsistent output

Repeated manual setup made it harder to reproduce formats across songs and platforms.

03
Product approach

Translate every editing decision into a reusable control.

I mapped the original production sequence, separated recurring decisions from song-specific inputs, and turned them into a browser workflow with reusable templates and a single preview-to-export path.

04
Features & decisions

Flexible where creators need choice. Structured where they need speed.

IMPORT

LRC and text import

Bring in timed LRC files, plain lyrics, or pasted text and continue into the built-in timing workflow.

TIME

Line- or word-level timing

Choose the timing precision that fits a scrolling, wave, or word-by-word format.

STYLE

Typography and color controls

Adjust fonts, hierarchy, colors, alignment, background, and lyric emphasis in one place.

FORMAT

Templates and aspect ratios

Reuse visual systems across scrolling, wave, and monochrome word-timed treatments.

LOCAL

Browser-local processing

Keep media processing local where supported, reducing handoffs and protecting source assets.

EXPORT

Background-ready export

Export can run separately after setup, freeing the creator from active frame-by-frame editing.

06
Role & process

Product ownership, with AI-assisted development.

I owned the problem definition, workflow mapping, feature priorities, interface decisions, testing, and iteration. AI-assisted development helped translate those decisions into a working browser product; it did not replace product judgment or production validation.

“The goal was not to automate creativity. It was to remove the repeated steps surrounding it.”
07
Reflection & next steps

A working tool is the start of product learning.

WHAT WORKED

Building from a real production workflow kept decisions practical. Reusable controls shortened active editing while preserving visual flexibility.

CURRENT LIMITATIONS

Browser encoding support and export performance vary by device, and advanced timing still benefits from careful review.

NEXT TO TEST

More creator testing, clearer first-use guidance, stronger preset management, and performance checks across browsers and lower-powered devices.
